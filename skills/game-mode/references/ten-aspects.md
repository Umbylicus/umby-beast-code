# The ten aspects

One critic per aspect. Each entry lists what the critic checks and what a 5, 7, and 9 look like. Paste the matching entry into that critic's brief.

## 1. Core loop and fun
**Check:** the main verb is clear in the first 30 seconds. The loop repeats every 10 to 20 seconds. Each loop holds a real choice. Failure is clear and the retry is quick. Something rewards the player each loop.
**5:** it works, but the player repeats one action with no decision. **7:** clear verb, a real choice each loop, fair retries. **9:** the loop escalates, choices interact, and the next run sounds more interesting than the last.

## 2. Controls and movement feel
**Check:** input shows on screen within a frame or two. Forgiveness windows where the genre expects them (late jumps, buffered inputs). Consistent acceleration and stopping. Hitboxes match what is drawn. The camera follows without jitter.
**5:** floaty or mushy, misses feel unfair. **7:** responsive and forgiving, no dropped inputs. **9:** small inputs give precise results, and a failure feels like the player's own mistake.

## 3. Visuals and art direction
**Check:** one style and palette across the whole game. Shapes readable at small size. Consistent pixel density or camera scale. Background, play layer, and interface separated by brightness or color. Original look.
**5:** mismatched assets, hard to read when busy. **7:** coherent palette, clear shapes, good contrast. **9:** one distinct visual idea used with restraint, with color and light guiding the eye.

## 4. Animation and feedback
**Check:** every hit, pickup, and action gets a visible reaction. Screen shake is short and rare. Pauses on impact are timed in seconds, not frames. Actions have a wind-up and a recovery. Visual and sound feedback land together.
**5:** some actions get no response. **7:** most actions respond, timing is consistent, effects are used in moderation. **9:** every action has layered feedback that scales with its impact.

## 5. Audio
**Check:** a distinct sound for each key action. Music loops with no click. Music does not bury effects. Volume and mute controls exist and persist. Sound starts after the first user input, as browsers require.
**5:** repetitive or unbalanced, or silent in places. **7:** distinct effects, clean loops, a balanced mix, working settings. **9:** audio responds to the state of play, with variation on repeated sounds.

## 6. Level and content design
**Check:** each level or encounter adds at most one new idea. A safe place to learn comes before the test. Pacing leaves room to breathe. Enemies, items, or cards differ in how they play, not only how they look.
**5:** filler, or one layout repeated. **7:** clear teaching, real variety, sensible pacing. **9:** every level or encounter has a purpose and a moment players remember.

## 7. Interface and onboarding
**Check:** the first action is taught by playing, not by a wall of text. The on-screen display shows only what matters now. Menus work by keyboard and controller. Pause, restart, and settings are easy to find. Text is readable on a phone.
**5:** cluttered, or the player has to guess. **7:** learned through play, clear display, usable menus. **9:** the player never wonders what to do next.

## 8. Difficulty and progression
**Check:** difficulty rises across a session. The curve is written in `docs/balance.md`. Upgrades change how the game plays, not only the numbers. Failing gives a reason to try again. Early success is likely enough to learn the game.
**5:** flat, or random spikes. **7:** a visible ramp and meaningful unlocks. **9:** several ways to grow stronger, a curve tested by play or simulation, fair at every point.

## 9. Performance and stability
**Check:** measured frame rate on a mid-range laptop profile and a phone profile. No console errors in normal play. Memory steady over five minutes. Loading shows progress. Resizing and switching tabs do not break the game.
**5:** runs, but drops frames during effects. **7:** steady at the target frame rate with no leaks in a short session. **9:** a written frame-time budget that is met, clean recovery from pause, resize, and lost focus.

## 10. Polish, originality, and replay value
**Check:** its own theme, names, and identity, with nothing lifted from another game. No placeholder text or debug display. Every screen and edge case finished. A reason to play again: seeds, unlocks, modes, or meaningful randomness.
**5:** generic theme, visible placeholders. **7:** finished, with original touches and a reason to return. **9:** clearly its own game, strong replay pull, no rough edges found.
